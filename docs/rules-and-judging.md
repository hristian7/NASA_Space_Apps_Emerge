## Deadlines (Sofia time)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Sat 14 Nov, 09:00 | NASA project submission opens; no work on the challenge before this |
| **Sun 15 Nov, 14:00** | **Sofia demo video due** (30–60 s) |
| Sun 15 Nov, 14:30 | Jury watches and scores every demo |
| Sun 15 Nov, 15:45 / 16:00 | Top 5 announced / live pitch: 3 min + 5 min Q&A (on site) |
| **Sun 15 Nov, 23:59** | **NASA submission closes** ("Submit for Judging" on the team's project page) |

## What NASA requires

- **Answer the official challenge** (this one counts).
- **Every team member** must be registered for the Sofia event, not waitlisted, and listed on the team page. No member changes after submission.
- **A demo,** either:
  - a **video of up to 30 s**, or
  - **up to 7 slides** (including title).
  - In English, or with English subtitles.
  - Hosted at a public link that needs no login.
  - **Make one 30-second video:** it fits both Sofia and NASA.
- **A link to the final project.** It must be public.
- **All form fields** filled in, in English.
- **References:** list every external resource (data, code, images, text), even your own earlier code.
- **SI (AI) usage note:** describe how SI (AI) tools were used. NASA calls AI "Super Intelligence (SI)".
- **Two boxes to tick:** "Terms and Conditions" and "Confirm All Work is Original".
- **No images, video, voices or likeness of anyone under 18.**

## Open source and reuse

- **Licence:** your new content is released as open source, **Apache 2.0 by default** if you don't choose another OSI-approved licence.
- **Non-NASA material** (data, code) must be freely available or open source.
- **Rights:** you must hold the rights to everything submitted. No export-controlled content.
- **Reusing earlier code:** allowed if you own it, list it, and publish it open source `[U]`. That is our reading; no rule names this case directly.

## How projects are judged

Each criterion is scored **1–5** at every stage. How the scores are combined is not published.

| Criterion | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| **Impact** | How big a problem it solves and how many people it could help or inspire |
| **Creativity** | How new and original the idea is, or how much it improves on what exists |
| **Validity** | Scientifically sound, actually works, easy to use |
| **Relevance** | Answers the challenge directly; **NASA data is a core part**, not decoration |
| **Presentation** | A clear, engaging story about the problem, solution and impact. Judged from the demo and project page. NASA says to give it equal effort |

## The path to an award

**Stages:** Sofia jury picks Global Nominees (announced 3 Dec 2026) → expert judges pick Finalists (17 Dec 2026) → NASA leaders pick 10 Global Winners (26 Jan 2027).

**Odds:** in 2025, about 1,290 nominees `[V]` became 45 finalists `[D]` and then 10 winners `[V]`.

**The 10 awards:** Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact.

**Most relevant here** `[U]`: **Local Impact**, **Best Use of Data**, **Galactic Impact**.
- **Earth/map-data projects won Local Impact in 2023, 2024 and 2025:**
  - 2023: imaging spectroscopy of a biodiversity hotspot;
  - 2024: Community Mapping;
  - 2025: BloomWatch (flowering seasons from satellite data).
- **A 2024 farm Earth-observation tool won Best Use of Data and Galactic Impact.**
