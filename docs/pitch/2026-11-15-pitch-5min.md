### Slide 4. The data problem: NASA's record broke in 2012 (1:05 to 1:30)

**On screen**
- Headline: **NASA's 25-year fire record broke in two in 2012.**
- Bar pair, whole world, 2013, same fires: **MODIS 3.7 M** fire cell-days vs **VIIRS 10.2 M**. Label: **×2.7. New satellite, not more fire.** `[V]`
- Sub: MODIS sees 1 km pixels since 2000. VIIRS sees 375 m since 2012. Joined naively, fire seems to triple when only the instrument changed.

**Visual:** the cliff chart from the 30 s video (same colours), animated.

**Script (Hristian)**
> And the data behind every fire map has a crack in it. In 2012 NASA launched a sharper satellite. It sees almost three times more fire than the old one, over the same ground. Put the two records together and fire seems to triple overnight. So nobody can honestly say whether this summer was unusual compared with 2007.

### Slide 5. What exists today (1:30 to 1:50)

**On screen**

|---|---|---|---|

| GWIS (EU JRC) | No, each sensor vs its own 10-year average | Countries | Fire danger, not scored |
| EFFIS | Burned areas from 2006, fires of about 30 ha+ | Europe and neighbours | No |

**Script (Hristian)**

The only fire calendar we found stops in 2022 and covers twenty countries. Nothing gives one record from 2000 to today for any point, plus a forecast that is checked against real fires.

## Backup slides (for questions, not presented)

### Backup: How the conversion works
- Squares of 0.25° (about 28 km), month by month.
- For each country, 5° latitude band and calendar month: how VIIRS relates to MODIS in the years both flew (fitted on 2012 to 2018), pulled towards the country and world average where data is thin, plus a small background term for months MODIS saw nothing `[V]`.
- Then Nov 2000 to early 2012 is converted to VIIRS level `[V]`.
- Across 203 countries with enough fire: middle country misses by about 40%; big fire countries about 22%; smallest third about 107% `[V]`.

### Backup: How the risk model works
- Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) rank forest and wildland squares `[V]`.
- Inputs: NASA's fire-weather forecast made 3 days ahead, earlier fire history, recent nearby fires, land type `[V]`.
- Only data up to two days before the forecast day `[V]`.
- Gain over NASA's index in Europe: 12.7 points, 95% interval 11.2 to 14.2 `[V]`.

### Backup: Why not 80 or 90%?
- Picking 5 squares at random already catches 39.4% in Europe, because some regions are small `[V]`.
- Published 80 to 90% figures come from tests with equal shares of fire and no-fire days; strict published measures for next-day ignitions are 8 to 25% `[D]`.
- We publish the baselines so the number can be judged.

### Backup: Data and AI
- 22 open data sources, 11 from NASA: FIRMS (MODIS, VIIRS), GFWED fire weather, IMERG rain, SMAP soil moisture, VIIRS greenness, MODIS land cover, POWER, GIBS `[V]`.
- AI coding assistants under the team's direction; expected answers written before the code by a separate agent; results checked against real satellite data ([AI-use record](../si-usage.md)).

### Backup: what doesn't work yet (only if asked)

**On screen**
- Headline: **What doesn't work yet.**
- 2023 to 2025, the years of satellite drift: Bulgaria's history error rises to about **56%** `[V]`.
- City squares are overstated: Sofia about **4×** its own history `[V]`.
- Low probabilities are not well calibrated, so we show levels, not exact chances `[V]`.
- **Risk outlook (unofficial).** A research prototype, not a warning system. Follow local authorities (112).

**Visual:** plain list, no decoration; the "unofficial" line in a box.

**Answer (Martin), if a judge asks about weaknesses**
> We publish the weak spots too. The newest years drift, cities are overstated, and low probabilities are rough, so we show levels, not exact chances. It's a research prototype, not a warning system.
