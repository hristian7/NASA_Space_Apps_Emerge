# NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: the 14 challenges at a glance

A short summary of every challenge, written from the official pages on 14 November 2026. The full NASA text and the official resource links for each one are in [challenges-full.md](challenges-full.md). Data sources explained in plain language: [data-sources.md](data-sources.md).

Almost every "consideration" on the challenge pages is phrased as "you may (but are not required to)". The real requirement is the core objective, which is what the table below lists.

## Key rules

- **Dates:** the hackathon runs 14 and 15 November 2026.
- **One challenge per team.** Each person can be on one team only, and a team works on one challenge.
- **No work before the event.** "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon."
- **Team size:** a Local Event team can be one person; a Universal (online) Event team needs at least two people to be eligible for global judging. Six people at most.
- **Submission:** through the team's project page on spaceappschallenge.org. Details are in the Project Submission Guide and the Judging and Awards Guide on the Resources page.
- **Judging:** local judges pick Global Nominees; NASA and partner-agency experts pick Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions; an executive committee picks 10 Global Winners. Nominees, finalists and mentions are announced in December 2026, winners in January 2027. Everyone who submits gets a participation certificate. No cash prize is listed; the recognition for 2026 winners was still to be announced.
- **Award categories** (from 2025): Most Inspirational, Best Mission Concept, Galactic Impact, Local Impact, Global Connection, Best Use of Technology, Best Use of Storytelling, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Science, Art and Technology.

## The challenges

| Challenge | Level | What to build | Main data |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/adapting-farms-with-nasa-data/) | Intermediate, Advanced | A crop-rotation decision tool combining NASA Earth observations with soil, crop and farmer priorities. Suggests comparing at least three rotations, including the current one, with evidence, benefits, gaps and risks for each. | SMAP, ECOSTRESS, GPM, Landsat, MODIS, NASA POWER, GRACE, SRTM, GEDI |
| [Dancing with the SARs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/dancing-with-the-sars/) | Intermediate, Advanced | An interactive app that uses NISAR radar data to track and visualise one or more kinds of surface change (floods, wildfire, subsidence, ice, farming) at a chosen place. The format is open: story map, video, dashboard. | NISAR, plus optical and GIS context layers |
| [Be An Earth System Trend Detective!](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/earth-system-trend-detective/) | Advanced | Pick an Earth science variable and region, plot a long time series and test whether the trend is significant (t-test, Mann-Kendall or confidence intervals). Doing both the visualisation and the analysis is the full scope. | MODIS, OMI, AIRS, GPM, MERRA-2, GLDAS |
| [Flame in Freefall](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall/) | Intermediate, Advanced | An AI-powered web dashboard that summarises, ranks and interprets NASA's microgravity combustion experiments to give fire-safety insights for spacecraft, stations and Moon or Mars bases. | NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) combustion data |
| [Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/health-monitoring-software/) | Intermediate | A visual tool where astronauts record health indicators (heart rate, blood pressure, sleep, mood and so on), assess their status and get guidance. A clickable body figure is one suggestion. | NASA Human Research Program, other agencies' health resources |
| [Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/interplanetary-survival-guide-martian-map/) | Intermediate | A layered map of one Marswalk location or route with at least three kinds of data, safety information (radiation, obstacles) and a route plan. Could be a tablet app, a helmet display or a habitat screen. | Mars Trek, USGS geologic maps, rover and orbiter data |
| [Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/) | Beginner/Youth, Intermediate | A game for children aged 7 to 12 who run a Moon or Mars outpost, trading off power, life support, radiation shielding and food. Every choice should cost something, grounded in real NASA data. A browser game with no login is preferred. | DONKI, OSDR, Artemis and Mars surface data |
| [Lunar Landing Site Navigator](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/lunar-landing-site-navigator/) | Intermediate, Advanced | A tool to compare lunar landing sites and dates quickly, showing the Sun and Earth against the horizon, solar elevation for power, Earth visibility for communication and time until sunset or Earthset. | LOLA terrain, JPL Horizons, Quickmap, Moon Trek, CLPS sites |
| [Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/modis-and-viirs-hot-spots/) | Intermediate, Advanced | First harmonise the MODIS and VIIRS fire detection records into one consistent series, then build a web app with a burning-activity calendar from the early 2000s to today for a chosen area, with comparisons to past years and a CSV download. | NASA FIRMS (MODIS and VIIRS) |
| [Abandoned but not Forgotten](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/nasas-discarded-equipment-storytelling/) | Beginner/Youth, Intermediate | A story-driven piece for school-age audiences about NASA hardware left on the Moon, on Mars and in deep space, and the science it made possible. Any format: web, game, illustrated book, video, podcast. Accuracy matters. | NASA mission archives and imagery |
| [Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Moon Base and Mars](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/) | All levels | Use open Earth, Moon and Mars data to find and characterise places on Earth (ideally lesser known) that resemble the Moon or Mars, explaining where the analogy holds and where it breaks. Output can be a map, similarity ranking, ML tool or report. | Earth, Moon and Mars data from NASA and other agencies, local data |
| [Planet X and SPHEREx](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/planet-x-and-spherex/) | Advanced | A public web tool: enter sky coordinates and see SPHEREx images of that patch play like a short video, so moving objects stand out. Test it on Barnard's Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855, RW Aurigae and R Draconis. | SPHEREx (IRSA), with optional Gaia and WISE overlays |
| [Space Mission Design Game](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/space-mission-design-game/) | Beginner/Youth, Intermediate | A game to design and run a whole space mission (objective, spacecraft, instruments, launcher, orbit) within mass, power, budget, data and communication limits. Single player or co-op for up to four, ending in a simulated result and score. | No specific dataset required |
| [The Earth Information Jukebox](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/the-earth-information-jukebox/) | Beginner/Youth, Intermediate | Turn NASA Earth Information Center visualisations into sound generated in real time. Accessibility for blind and low-vision users is the core theme. Can be an interactive app or a curated set of generated audio tracks. | NASA Earth Information Center visualisations |
