# 2026 fire data, from the firms-catalog mirror (14 Nov 2026)

NASA's yearly country files stop on 31 Dec 2025, so 2026 comes from the public firms-catalog mirror of FIRMS (one GeoParquet file per year, updated hourly). `pipeline/build_cells_catalog.py` turns a mirror year into the same cell-day table `pipeline/build_cells.py` produces, with one column added, `provisional`, true where the row came from near-real-time data. The mirror's own `quality` column says which rows those are, and measured on 2026 it marks exactly the rows that have no `type` `[V]`. Tags: `[V]` measured here, `[D]` directional.

## What was produced, and whether it agrees with the existing table

`data/derived/world/cell_days_2026.parquet`: **28,413,055 cell-days from 42,874,393 cleaned detections** `[V]`, 1 Jan to 14 Nov 2026. Its columns and types are identical to the existing table, same order, plus `provisional` `[V]`. Cleaning is identical: vegetation fires only, MODIS confidence 30 or more, VIIRS confidence `n` or `h`, valid coordinates, FRP 0 or more. The mirror carries no latitude or longitude columns, so both come from the point geometry. The last two columns check the same script against the existing table on 2025, a year both sources hold.

**S-NPP stops on 2 Nov 2026, confirmed** `[V]`; every later day rests on NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. Detections agree as closely (+0.01% to +0.07% `[V]`). The mirror is a hair higher because NASA's country files are clipped to country borders and drop points outside every country, which the mirror's notes state and our table shows as 0.92% of 2025 detections in no country `[V]`. Nothing was adjusted to make anything match.

Per country the gap looks much bigger (Bulgaria 5.7% to 11.6% lower, the USA 1.9% to 3.6% lower `[V]`) and it is **not** a data difference. NASA labels each detection with its own country; the mirror has no country column, so a row can only take the country of its 0.25 degree cell, and a border cell moves to one side whole. Measured on the same cells, the gap falls to 0.5% to 1.3% for Bulgaria and 0.05% for the USA `[V]`.

## The static-source mask (the empty-`type` trap)

Near-real-time rows carry no `type`, so gas flares and industrial heat cannot be dropped by label. The mask was built from the raw yearly files (all years, all sensors, before cleaning): a 0.01 degree cell is masked when it holds 10 detections or more and more than half are `type` 2, "other static land source". 207,129 cells ever held a `type` 2 detection; **30,883 are masked** `[V]`, in `data/derived/world/static_mask.parquet`. A row with no `type` is dropped when it falls in a masked cell and kept otherwise.

| Evidence that the mask works | Result |
|---|---|
| Share of 2025 `type` 2 detections inside masked cells | **93.2%** `[V]` |
| Share of 2025 vegetation (`type` 0) detections inside masked cells, the cost | 0.40% `[V]` |
| Same two, on Jan to Jun 2026 science-quality rows, which the mask never saw | 89.8% caught, 0.44% cost `[V]` |
| 2026 rows that passed cleaning and the mask then dropped | 6.46% of near-real-time rows, 0.49% of science-quality rows `[V]` |

That 6.46% sits just under the 7.4% share `type` 2 rows make up of labelled 2026 rows `[V]`, so the mask removes most of what the label would have removed.

## Limits: what would mislead if the 2026 months were shown as they are

- **November 2026 is unfinished** (14 days of 30) and for S-NPP holds only 2 days `[V]`. It must never be compared with a whole past month.
- **Provisional share by month** `[V]`: 30% for Jan to Apr, 61.5% for May, 90.4% for June, 100% from July on. Science-quality data lags about four months and replaces those rows, so recent months can be revised. NOAA-21 is near-real-time for the whole of 2026 `[V]`.
- **2.32% of 2026 detections (1.51% of cell-days) have no country** `[V]`, against 0.92% in 2025. The cell-to-country table knows only the 176,415 cells that had fire through 2025, so a 2026 fire in a cell that never burned before comes out `Unassigned`, and per-country 2026 totals undercount by up to about 2% `[D]`.
- The mask is built from history, so a gas flare lit for the first time in 2026 is not in it `[D]`.

## How to reproduce

The year step prints its row counts, then a table of hard limits: future dates, days outside the year, counts below 1, negative or missing FRP, cells off the map, unknown satellites. Every one was 0 for both 2025 and 2026 `[V]`.

```bash
mkdir -p data/raw/firms-catalog   # 1.9 GB for 2025 and 2026 together
curl -C - -o data/raw/firms-catalog/detections_2026.parquet https://data.source.coop/portolan-mirrors/firms-catalog/detections/year=2026/detections.parquet
python3 pipeline/build_cells_catalog.py mask              # 1 min 40 s here [V], reads the raw yearly files
python3 pipeline/build_cells_catalog.py year --year 2026  # 6 s here [V]
```
